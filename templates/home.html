About the Project

Why CAPTURED?

With the rise of large language models, students increasingly rely on AI tools to answer mathematical questions without critically evaluating the responses. CAPTURED was built to change that.

🎯
Expose Overconfidence
AI models report high confidence scores even when their answers are wrong. CAPTURED makes this disconnect immediately visible to every student.
🔍
Step-by-Step Verification
Every reasoning step the AI produces is independently verified using Wolfram Alpha catching exactly where the logic breaks down.
📚
Build Critical Thinking
The three-button flow forces students to engage with AI output rather than passively accept it. Building the habit of verification.
📊
Teacher Analytics
A persistent dashboard tracks error rates, hallucination counts, and confidence patterns across sessions. Giving educators real data.

How to Use

Three steps. One insight.

The interaction reveals information progressively — you commit to seeing an answer before you can verify it.

STEP 01
Pick a Question
Select from 45 curated math problems. Filter by topic using the pill buttons. The question prompt is shown before any AI is called.
STEP 02
Generate an Answer
Choose how the AI responds — with or without reasoning shown.
⚡ Quick Answer ◯ With Steps
STEP 03
Check the Answer
Wolfram Alpha verifies every step. The answer is compared to ground truth. The hallucination alert fires if confidence was high but wrong.
✓ Check Answer
STEP 04
Read the Results
Steps are colored green or red. The confidence bar shows what the AI claimed. Look for the pattern — confidence does not equal correctness.
The Core Finding

The Hallucination Danger Zone

When an AI model expresses high confidence but answers incorrectly, that is the hallucination danger zone. CAPTURED makes this visible every time it happens.

!
High Confidence + Wrong Answer
GPT-4o-mini consistently reports confidence between 85–95% regardless of correctness. On LLM Trap questions — Monty Hall, harmonic mean speed, Bayes theorem — the AI answers with near-certainty and gets it wrong. CAPTURED catches this every time and shows exactly where the reasoning failed.
